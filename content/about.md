## Hi, I'm Otmane 👋

I live most of my life in a terminal. Not as an aesthetic choice — as an actual, literal fact about where my attention goes when nobody's asking anything of me. Give me a shell prompt, a stack of GNU tools, and something worth understanding, and I'm content in a way I rarely am anywhere else.

### Linux is at the core of everything

I'm a sysadmin by instinct and a Linux kernel contributor by ambition — small patches so far, but the trajectory matters more than the current position. I've fallen completely, unreasonably in love with this operating system: not just what it does, but how it's built — thousands of strangers across decades, arguing in mailing lists, reviewing each other's work line by line, converging on something none of them could have built alone. That's not just software to me. It's proof that a community can build something built to outlast any of its individual contributors, including me. I want to spend my life adding to it, even in small ways. Especially in small ways — the kernel doesn't need heroes, it needs people who show up and do the careful, unglamorous work.

Linus Torvalds is the closest thing I have to an idol, and not for the reasons people assume. It's not the fame or the famously blunt mailing-list history. It's that he started something as a hobby project, gave it away, and then spent decades doing the harder thing: stepping back enough to let a process outgrow its founder. That's a rarer skill than writing the first version of anything.

### Perl and Raku, or: why I still write code for fun

Perl is where I go when I want a language that doesn't pretend to be tidy. It's expressive to the point of being dangerous, and I mean that as the highest compliment — a good one-liner still feels like a small act of craftsmanship to me, the programming equivalent of a well-turned sentence. Raku is the opposite kind of joy: deliberate, exploratory, willing to let you redesign the language itself if the built-in grammar doesn't fit your problem. Between the two of them I get both ends of what makes programming worth doing — Perl for getting something real done quickly, Raku for remembering that "the way things are usually done" is a choice, not a law of nature.

### Running and walking

I'm addicted to both, and I don't think that word is an exaggeration. There's a specific kind of clarity that only shows up after several kilometers — the mental chatter runs out of things to say before your legs run out of road, and what's left underneath is quiet enough to actually think in. Walking gives me the slow version of that: a problem I've been stuck on for hours in front of a screen will often just quietly resolve itself somewhere around the second kilometer of a walk I took to stop thinking about it. Running gives me the intense version — pushing a system (my own body, for once, instead of somebody's kernel module) right up against its limits and learning exactly where they are. Marathon training and kernel work have more in common than they look like: both reward patience, both punish shortcuts, and both quietly make you a more honest judge of your own limits.

### What fills the rest of the time

I listen to podcasts and read constantly — books most of all, and science fiction above everything else, because good science fiction is really just philosophy wearing a spacesuit, asking "what if" about technology, society, and human nature all at once. I read poetry too, in smaller doses, for the opposite reason: where science fiction expands the question, poetry compresses the answer into as few words as it can survive on. And when I want to switch my brain off entirely, there's always a Marvel movie — no apologies, no ironic distance, just a genuine soft spot for people in capes solving problems with punching that I'd solve with a patch.

### The internet is my hometown

I mean that literally, not as a joke. I got my Baccalauréat in Mathematics, then spent two years in CPGE before life pointed me toward the terminal instead — but long before any of that, the internet was already the place I actually lived. It's less a website and more a country: a sprawling, half-lawless, entirely voluntary club for nerds, hackers, and developers who found each other because nowhere else quite fit. Mailing lists, IRC-shaped communities, plain-text blogs, RSS feeds — that's the internet I grew up in, and it's the one I still believe in: decentralized, a little rough around the edges, built by people instead of extracted from them.

I'm based in **Kenitra, Morocco**, and I love it — genuinely the best city in the world to me. But if you ask where I'm *really* from, my honest answer is still the internet. That's home.

### A few more things, for the record

I'm also a **marathon runner** and a **writer** — the running clears my head, the writing is what I do with whatever's left over afterward. I'm a **privacy and open source advocate** and, in whatever small way one person can be, an **internet activist**: I believe the internet is worth defending as a commons, not just using as a product. And underneath all of it, I'm an **introvert** — which probably explains a lot of the rest of this page. The terminal, the long solitary runs, the books instead of the party: not a phase, just how I'm built.
